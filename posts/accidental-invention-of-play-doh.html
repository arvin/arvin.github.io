The accidental invention of Play-Doh

July 12, 2020

A story that I hold near my heart that's fraught with learnings is the creation of Play-Doh. Most of us can remember the smell of a fresh can from our childhood. But the journey of creating this toy takes many twists and turns that may surprise you.

The story begins as a soap company is going under in the late 1920s. The company, Kutol, sold powdered hand soap which was dwindling in demand. Cleo McVicker—a 21-year-old employee at the time—was tasked with selling off the company's assets as it was winding down its operations. Cleo managed to keep the company afloat and had enough of a profit to bring on his brother Noah.

The brothers learned that the executives at Kroger grocery stores were looking for a wallpaper cleaning product to help people remove the soot their coal-burning furnaces left on their walls. Coal was the leading way to heat one's home at the time, but the downside was that it would leave a layer of soot that was hard to clean. Without having the product or the know-how to actually make it, the brothers took an order for 15,000 cases with a substantial penalty if they couldn't deliver it on time. The company didn't have enough money to cover the penalty if they failed to deliver, but the brothers managed to fulfill the order after Noah found a formula used to create similar products.

After WWII, the invention of Vinyl Wallpaper—which could be easily cleaned with soap and water—meant that people weren't buying the company's wallpaper cleaner anymore. Their market was slowly dwindling, and the company had to go back to the drawing board yet again.

As Noah and his nephew Joe, who joined the team after Cleo's death, scrambled to keep Kutol afloat, little did they know that the owner of a nursery school would help them find the right market. Kay Zufall, the sister-in-law of Joe, was using the wallpaper cleaner as a cheap material to have her kids make Christmas decorations. Knowing that the company was in financial turmoil, she called Joe and told him about how the children were using the cleaner as a toy. Upon learning this, Joe rewrote the formula removing the detergent and replacing it with that fresh almond smell we've all grown to love. They started selling their product to schools throughout Cincinnati and it was a hit. Eventually, they partnered with the Captain Kangaroo production company which helped them market the product nationwide, and sales steadily increased.

There was a lot of struggle in the story of Play-Doh—from the diminishing markets and bold risk-taking to surprise deaths. But the story is so rich with lessons that I often think back to it when I struggle.

My takeaways

This story demonstrates the importance of talking to people and finding out those specific use cases. Kay stumbled upon using the wallpaper cleaner as a toy in a magazine, but not many people would have figured that out themselves.

Being empathetic towards how people live their lives and their day-to-day will make your life a lot easier. Each person is a unique puzzle that may share some common pieces. Finding such problems is the hard part and conversation is the act of sifting through potential pieces.

Being hopeful is a superpower, especially in business. Taking something that isn't working and flipping it takes tenacity and grit. The brothers had no background in the soap business and had no understanding of wallpaper cleaner prior to selling it to Kroger. But they stayed hopeful and pushed themselves to adapt to change when times were tough instead of calling it quits.
